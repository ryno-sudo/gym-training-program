12-Week Training Program

Lean recomp · 90kg → +5kg lean · 48yo intermediate · L5/S1-safe · Revo Charlestown

1. Overview & profile-driven choices

UPDATED for torn + bulging + prolapsed L5/S1 — get physio clearance before week 1:
  • NO conventional deadlifts, back squats with bar on spine, bent-over barbell rows, RDLs, good mornings, hyperextensions with weight, OR flat bench press.
  • NO hip hinges under load — period. Cable pull-through and Romanian-style work are out until cleared by a physio. Hip thrust is allowed (no spinal load) and lying leg curls cover the hamstring work.
  • NO spinal flexion under load. No crunches, no sit-ups, no Russian twists, no weighted hanging leg/knee raises. Mid-core work is anti-extension, anti-rotation, anti-lateral-flexion only.
  • Squats are MACHINE-supported only: leg press as primary (full spine support), hack squat as secondary (some axial load — start light, brace hard).
  • Daily dead hang from pull-up bar (30-60 sec) at the end of every session — passive spinal decompression, McGill-recommended for bulging discs.
  • 2x/week frequency per muscle group via Upper/Lower split.
  • Sessions capped at 50-65 min to fit your 45-75 min window.

RED FLAGS — go to ED immediately: saddle numbness (numbness around groin/inner thighs), leg weakness, foot drop, loss of bladder/bowel control. These are signs of cauda equina syndrome and are surgical emergencies.

Before you start week 1:
  1. Get current MRI (within last 12 months) so you and your physio know what you're working with.
  2. 1-2 sessions with a physiotherapist who specialises in disc rehab — get them to watch you do hack squat, hip thrust, chest-supported row.
  3. If they say no, listen to them. This program adapts.

What this program is built on: Schoenfeld 2017 meta-analysis on volume-hypertrophy relationship. Helms RIR system (2018). Israetel volume landmarks (MV/MEV/MAV/MRV). Krieger 2010 meta on frequency. Mike Israetel and RP's set-volume research. NOT bro-science Instagram templates.

What MEV/MAV mean: MEV = Minimum Effective Volume (sets needed to grow at all). MAV = Maximum Adaptive Volume (sets where growth peaks). Above MAV you cross into MRV (Maximum Recoverable Volume) and start overtraining. Your peptide stack lifts your MRV slightly, but at 48 with eGFR concerns we stay closer to MEV-MAV midpoint, NOT pushing MRV.

Frequency: hitting each muscle 2x/week

Schoenfeld et al. (2016) meta-analysis on frequency: training a muscle 2x/week produces ~3.1% more growth than 1x/week at matched volume. The split is built around this — every muscle gets at least one direct + one indirect session per week.

RIR (Reps in Reserve) system — autoregulation explained

RIRWhat it feels likeWhen to use
3+ RIR"I could've done 3+ more reps easy"Warm-up sets, deload weeks, technique-focus sets
2 RIR"I could've done 2 more"Foundation phase compound + most isolation work
1 RIR"Maybe one more, with grinding"Building phase last set, Peak phase working sets
0 RIRTrue failureLast set of isolation only in Peak phase. NEVER on compounds (form breakdown = injury).
Why RIR beats fixed % of 1RM: Your strength fluctuates day-to-day (Reta-suppressed days, sleep, stress, injection days). Fixed % programs ignore this. RIR autoregulates — you adjust load to hit the target effort level. On a great day you'll lift more; on a bad day, less. Either way, the stimulus is right.

3. 12-Week Periodisation

PhaseWeeksFocusSets / Reps / RPE
Foundation1-4Volume accumulation, form, mind-muscle connection3-4 sets · 10-12 reps compound, 12-15 isolation · RPE 7 (3 RIR)
Deload5Recovery, supercompensation2 sets · same reps · RPE 5 (5 RIR) · 60% load
Building6-9Strength-hypertrophy, heavier loads4 sets compound, 3 isolation · 8-10 reps compound, 10-12 isolation · RPE 8 (2 RIR)
Deload10Recovery before peak2 sets · same reps · RPE 5 · 60% load
Peak / Intensification11-12Maximum stimulus, intensification techniques4-5 sets compound, 3 isolation · 6-8 reps compound, 8-12 isolation · RPE 8-9 (1-2 RIR) + drop sets on last set isolation
RPE explained: RPE = Rate of Perceived Exertion. RIR = Reps in Reserve.
  • RPE 7 = 3 reps left in the tank when you stop the set
  • RPE 8 = 2 reps left
  • RPE 9 = 1 rep left
  • RPE 10 = absolute failure
For your goal (hypertrophy + recovery on Reta), never train to failure on compounds. Save it for last set of isolation only in Peak phase.

Weekly progression rule (during each phase)

  1. Week 1 of phase: Establish baseline weight at the prescribed RPE.
  2. Week 2: Add 1-2 reps at same weight, OR add 2.5kg if hit top of rep range.
  3. Week 3: Add 1-2 more reps at same weight, OR add another 2.5kg.
  4. Week 4: Push for one more rep on each set, OR add load.

Double progression: hit top of rep range with good form for 2 sessions in a row → add weight, drop back to bottom of rep range, build back up.

4. Day A — Upper Push

Chest · Shoulders (incl. overhead press) · Triceps · Mid-core

Estimated time: 55-65 min · Rest periods listed per exercise · Tempo notation: eccentric-pause-concentric-pause (e.g. 3-1-1-0 = 3sec down, 1sec pause, 1sec up, no pause top)

A1Incline Dumbbell Press

4 sets × 8-10 repsTempo 3-1-1-0Rest 90sBench at 30°
Cue: Squeeze the dumbbells at the top, don't smash them together. Elbows track at 45° from torso, not flared 90°. Pause at the bottom — chest stretch is where growth happens.
Why: Upper chest (clavicular pec) is the area most lifters under-develop and what makes a chest look "full" on a 48-year-old. DBs over barbell for shoulder safety + range of motion.
Watch form (YouTube)

A2Seated Machine Chest Press (vertical) OR Hammer Strength Incline Press

3 sets × 10-12 repsTempo 2-1-1-1Rest 90sMid-chest, no flat bench
Cue: Seated upright machine — back fully supported by pad. Drive handles forward and slightly up. Squeeze pecs at the top for 1 sec. Don't lock elbows.
Why: Replaces flat bench entirely. Vertical machine = your spine is supported the whole time, zero risk to your disc. Same chest stimulus, none of the lying-flat issue. If Revo only has flat Hammer Strength press, use seated cable chest press instead.
Substitution if no machine available: Standing cable chest press (chest-height cables, slight forward lean from hips with neutral spine).
Watch form (YouTube)

A3Cable Crossover (high-to-low)

3 sets × 12-15 repsTempo 2-1-2-1Rest 60sLower chest + inner
Cue: Slight forward lean, soft elbows fixed throughout. Bring hands down and across the body, finishing at hip level. Squeeze for full second at bottom.
Why: Stretch + squeeze in one move. Hits lower chest fibres + inner pec (sternal head) you can't get from pressing alone.
Watch form (YouTube)

A4Seated Dumbbell Lateral Raise

4 sets × 12-15 repsTempo 2-0-1-1Rest 60sLateral delt
Cue: Lead with elbows, not hands. Stop at shoulder height — going higher recruits traps. Slight forward lean (15°). Pinky slightly higher than thumb at top (like pouring a jug).
Why: Lateral delt is what gives shoulders their width. Most lifters do this wrong and end up training traps. Seated removes leg drive cheating.
Watch form (YouTube)

A5Seated Machine Shoulder Press (or Seated DB Overhead Press)

3 sets × 8-12 repsTempo 3-1-1-0Rest 90sAnterior delt + lateral delt — direct overhead stimulus
Cue: Back fully supported by the pad — do NOT arch the lumbar to press. Drive the handles straight up, not forward. Stop just short of lockout at the top. Slow 3-second descent — resist the weight, don't drop it. Keep elbows tracking slightly in front of the body, not flared directly to the sides.
Why (added — was missing): Incline and flat chest pressing does not adequately load the anterior deltoid or drive overall shoulder mass the way overhead pressing does. The angle is too horizontal. If you want wider, rounder shoulders, you need overhead pressing. Machine or seated DB version keeps your spine fully supported — zero axial load — completely safe for L5/S1. Rear delt work has been moved to Day C where it sits alongside Face Pulls for better volume grouping.
Substitution: If no machine available — Seated DB Overhead Press with a vertical back-supported bench (NOT standing). Start 16-20kg DBs, own the form before adding load.
Watch form (YouTube)

A6Overhead Cable Tricep Extension (rope)

3 sets × 10-12 repsTempo 3-1-1-1Rest 75sLong head
Cue: Elbows stay locked in position pointing forward. At top, separate the rope hands — this maximises long head contraction. Big stretch at the bottom — that's where the growth is.
Why: Long head is 60% of tricep mass and only worked when the shoulder is flexed (arm overhead). Skip this and your triceps look flat from the side.
Watch form (YouTube)

A7Tricep Pushdown (V-bar, supinated grip on last set)

3 sets × 12-15 repsTempo 2-0-1-1Rest 60sMedial + lateral head
Cue: Elbows pinned to ribs the entire time. Drive hands down and BACK at the bottom (slight extension behind hip line). Last set: flip to underhand grip for medial head emphasis.
Watch form (YouTube)

A8Pallof Press (mid-core)

3 sets × 10 reps each sideTempo 1-2-1-0Rest 30sAnti-rotation
Cue: Stand side-on to cable stack, chest height. Press cable straight out from chest, hold 2sec, return. Body should NOT rotate — that's the whole point. Brace abs hard.
Why: Anti-rotation is what protects the L5/S1 disc in real life (lifting, twisting). Most important core exercise for your back. Skip crunches forever.
Watch form (YouTube)

A9Dead Bug (mid-core)

3 sets × 8 reps each sideTempo slow + controlledRest 30sAnti-extension
Cue: Lower back pressed firmly into the floor the WHOLE time. If your back arches, you've gone too far. Exhale on the extension. Move slowly.
Why: Trains the deep transverse abdominis to brace the spine — the muscle that protects your disc when you stand up from a chair or lift a box.

5. Day B — Lower (Quad Focus)

Quads · Glutes · Calves · L5/S1-protected

Estimated time: 55-70 min · Leg press is the primary lift (fully supported spine). Hack squat is secondary with caveats.

B1Leg Press (low foot position for quads) — PRIMARY

4 sets × 10-12 repsTempo 3-1-1-0Rest 2 minQuad mass, full spine support
Cue: Feet low on platform = quad emphasis. Feet shoulder-width. STOP descent before your lower back rounds off the pad — this is the critical safety point. Your back must stay PRESSED into the pad the entire rep. Don't lock knees hard at the top.
Why this is now PRIMARY (was B2): With a torn disc, the leg press is the safest heavy quad builder. Spine fully supported in reclined position — zero compression. This is where you load heavy.
Watch form (YouTube)

B2Hack Squat (machine) — SECONDARY, lighter

3 sets × 10-15 repsTempo 3-1-1-0Rest 90sQuad emphasis, light load
Cue: Feet shoulder-width, slightly forward of hips. Brace abs HARD before each rep. Pause 1sec at the bottom. STOP if you feel anything in your low back.
Why secondary now (was B1): Hack squat still has some axial load through the spine even with the pad — for a torn annulus this is more risk than leg press. Use lighter loads (RPE 7), higher reps (10-15) for a quad pump rather than heavy strain. Skip entirely if your physio says no.
Substitution if uncomfortable: Goblet squat to box (light KB held at chest, sit back to a box at parallel — depth controlled, neutral spine throughout).
Watch form (YouTube)

B3Bulgarian Split Squat (DB)

3 sets × 10-12 reps each legTempo 2-0-1-0Rest 75s between sidesSingle-leg quad + glute
Cue: Front foot far enough forward that knee tracks over ankle (NOT past toes). Back foot on bench laces-down. Drive through front heel. Slight forward torso lean = more quad. Vertical torso = more glute.
Why: Single-leg work is the most underrated mass builder for legs and glutes. Also bullet-proofs hips and knees for everyday life. Lower load needed = less back stress.

B4Reverse Lunge (DB or holding rack for support)

3 sets × 10 reps each legTempo controlledRest 75sQuad + glute, less impact than walking lunge
Cue: Step BACKWARD into a lunge (less impact on the spine than the stepping-forward of walking lunges). Drop straight down, vertical torso, drive back up through front heel. Hold light DBs OR if balance is a challenge, hold the squat rack with one hand for support and use a single DB in the other.
Why changed: Walking lunges have a subtle spine-loading impact each step. Reverse lunges are gentler on the spine and easier to brace. Same muscle stimulus.

B5Leg Extension (single-leg on last set)

3 sets × 12-15 repsTempo 2-1-2-1Rest 60sQuad isolation, all heads
Cue: Pause at top for 1 second — squeeze quads hard. Slight toe-out for VMO emphasis (teardrop above knee). Last set single-leg for asymmetry correction.

B6Standing Calf Raise (Smith machine or machine)

4 sets × 10-15 repsTempo 2-2-1-1Rest 45sGastrocnemius
Cue: FULL range — let the heels drop deep below platform, then drive up to absolute peak. Pause 1 second at top. Pause 2 seconds at bottom stretch. Calves are stubborn — they need the stretch.

B7Seated Calf Raise

3 sets × 12-20 repsTempo 2-2-1-1Rest 45sSoleus
Cue: Same full range. Soleus needs higher reps (slow-twitch fibre). Burn out the last set — you should want to stop 2 reps before you do.

B8Suitcase Carry (LIGHT to start)

3 sets × 30m each sideTempo brisk walkRest 60sAnti-lateral flexion
Cue: Start with a 12-16kg DB or KB only — half what you think you can carry. Walk tall. Do NOT lean to the loaded side — your obliques fight that lean. Stop the set if your trunk starts to bend. Build slowly over weeks.
Why: Trains the lateral core (quadratus lumborum, obliques) which protect the disc during everyday asymmetric loads (carrying tools, kids, briefcase). With a torn disc, this is rehabilitative — but only with light weight initially. Skip if your physio says no carries during acute symptom flares.

6. Day C — Upper Pull

Back · Rear Delt · Traps · Biceps · Forearms · Mid-core

Estimated time: 60-70 min · Chest-supported rows replace bent-over rows. Lats hit from vertical AND horizontal vectors. Rear delt + trap work consolidated here from Day A for better volume grouping.

C1Lat Pulldown (wide, slight lean back)

4 sets × 8-12 repsTempo 3-1-1-1Rest 90sLats (width)
Cue: Grip just outside shoulders. Drive elbows down to ribs (not pull bar to chest). Squeeze lats hard at the bottom for 1 second. Slight backward lean (15°). Control the negative.
Why: Vertical pulling for lat width. Slight lean = bigger ROM and more lat involvement than fully upright.
Watch form (YouTube)

C2Chest-Supported DB Row (or Hammer Strength row)

4 sets × 8-12 repsTempo 2-1-1-1Rest 90sMid-back, NO spinal load
Cue: Bench at 30°, chest pinned to pad the entire set. Drive elbows back behind ribcage. Squeeze shoulder blades together at the top for 1 second. DON'T jerk or use momentum.
Why: This replaces bent-over barbell rows for you. Chest-supported = zero spinal load = safe for L5/S1. Same back stimulus, none of the risk. Hits rhomboids + mid traps + lats.
Watch form (YouTube)

C3Single-Arm Cable Row (low pulley)

3 sets × 10-12 reps each sideTempo 2-1-1-1Rest 60s/sideLat thickness + lower trap
Cue: Half-kneeling or standing split stance. Pull elbow back along ribs. Get a deep stretch at the front of the rep — let the shoulder blade come forward. Then squeeze hard at the back.
Watch form (YouTube)

C4Face Pull (rope, high cable)

4 sets × 12-15 repsTempo 2-2-1-1Rest 60sRear delt + rotator cuff + mid traps
Cue: Rope at forehead height. Pull rope ENDS apart and back — your hands should end up beside your ears, not in front. External rotation at the end. Pause 2 seconds.
Why: Best shoulder health exercise on the planet. With all the pressing on Day A, this keeps your rotator cuff balanced. Also hits rear delts and mid traps hard.
Watch form (YouTube)

C5Reverse Pec Deck (or Cable Reverse Fly)

3 sets × 12-15 repsTempo 2-1-1-1Rest 60sRear delt — moved from Day A, consolidated here
Cue: Imagine pulling a sword out of a sheath behind you. Squeeze shoulder blades apart, NOT together (this is rear delt, not rhomboids). Thumbs back at end position. Do NOT let your traps take over — keep the movement in the posterior delt.
Why moved to Day C: Rear delt work belongs on pull day alongside face pulls — both are horizontal pulling movements for the same muscle group. Consolidating them here lets Day A run a full overhead shoulder press instead, which was the gap in the program.
Watch form (YouTube)

C6DB or Machine Shrugs

3 sets × 10-15 repsTempo 1-2-1-0Rest 60sUpper traps — was listed in overview, was missing from all days
Cue: Hold DBs at sides (or use machine). Drive shoulders STRAIGHT UP — not forward, not rolling. Hold the top for 2 full seconds, squeezing the upper traps hard. Lower slowly. No bouncing, no momentum. Heavy enough to feel it, light enough to control.
Why (added — was genuinely missing): The original overview table promised "Traps: shrugs" but shrugs appeared in zero of the four days. This is the fix. Upper trap development is critical for the thick, rounded look across the top of the back — the muscle that bridges your neck to your shoulders. Without it, the back looks flat from behind regardless of how good your lats are.

C7Incline Dumbbell Curl

3 sets × 10-12 repsTempo 3-1-1-1Rest 60sBicep long head — peak builder
Cue: Bench at 60°. Let arms hang STRAIGHT down at the bottom — don't cheat the stretch. Curl up without swinging the elbows forward. Squeeze hard at the top.
Why: Long head of biceps is most active when the shoulder is in extension (arm behind body) — incline curl puts you there. Builds the bicep peak.
Watch form (YouTube)

C8Cable Curl — Arm Elevated (Coracobrachialis focus)

2 sets × 12-15 reps each sideTempo 2-1-2-1Rest 45sCoracobrachialis + bicep short head
Cue: Set cable to approximately shoulder height (or slightly above). Stand side-on or face the stack, raise your working arm forward and up so your elbow is at roughly shoulder height pointing toward the cable. Curl your hand toward your face/shoulder — the forearm moves while the upper arm stays elevated and fixed. Squeeze hard at peak contraction. Slow return. Do both sides.
Why (replaces Preacher Curl — added for coracobrachialis): The coracobrachialis runs from the coracoid process to the inner humerus — it assists shoulder flexion and arm adduction. It's most active when the arm is elevated forward during a curl, which standard curls, preacher curls and incline curls don't provide. This movement specifically targets it. The short head also gets worked here due to the forward arm position. 2 sets only — it's a small muscle, it doesn't need volume, it needs the right angle.

C9Hammer Curl (DB or rope cable) — superset with Reverse Wrist Curl

3 sets × 12 repsTempo 2-0-1-1Rest 30sBrachialis + brachioradialis + forearm extensors
Cue: Neutral grip (thumbs up). DBs slightly across body for extra brachialis. Don't swing. Go straight into the reverse wrist curl with no rest — different muscle group, zero recovery needed.
Why: Brachialis underneath the bicep makes the upper arm look thicker from the side — it's the muscle that pushes the bicep up. Brachioradialis builds the outer forearm. The muscle on the outside of your upper arm that you're looking to develop — this is it.

C10Wrist Curl + Reverse Wrist Curl (seated, DB or barbell)

3 rounds × 15-20 reps eachTempo 2-1-1-1Rest 45sForearm flexors + extensors
Cue: Forearms flat on thighs or bench. Let weight roll to fingertips at bottom for max stretch. Curl up using only wrist. Then flip to overhand for reverse — these will burn.
Why: Forearm size and grip strength = practical strength + better lifts everywhere else.
Watch form (YouTube)

C11Dead Hang (passive spinal decompression)

3 sets × 30-60 secTempo passive holdRest 60sSpinal traction + grip
Cue: Hang from pull-up bar, feet off floor (or just barely brushing). RELAX through the shoulders — let gravity stretch the spine. Bend knees if needed. Breathe deeply. Do NOT pull up or curl your knees — pure passive hang.
Why: Pure dead hang gives grip work + spine traction (rehabilitative for bulging discs) without any spinal flexion. McGill-recommended. Do this every single session — it takes 2 minutes and protects your disc.

11. Deload Weeks (5 & 10)

Deloads are when you actually grow. Lifting breaks the muscle down. Recovery rebuilds it bigger. Skipping deloads = injury at 48, especially with the eGFR concern. Don't be a hero.

Deload protocol

  • Same exercises, same days — keep the rhythm
  • 50% volume — half the sets (e.g. 4 sets becomes 2 sets)
  • 60% load — if you were lifting 100kg, drop to 60kg
  • Same reps, same tempo — focus on perfect form
  • RPE 5 — should feel almost too easy
  • NO finishers, NO extras — get in, do the work, get out
  • Skip the optional cardio finisher
  • Sleep 8+ hours if possible — this is where the magic happens

Signs you NEED to deload (don't wait for week 5/10 if you see these)

  • Resting heart rate elevated 5+ bpm above your baseline for 3+ days
  • Sleep getting worse despite DSIP
  • Mood dropping, irritability up
  • Joint aches that don't resolve in 48 hours
  • Top set of last week's lift now feels like a struggle at the same weight
  • Loss of training motivation lasting more than 2 sessions
  • Minor injuries or niggles starting to show up

12. Intensity Techniques (when to use, when they backfire)

Cardinal rule: Intensity techniques are stress multipliers. Use them when you've already optimised volume, frequency, RIR and form. Adding them too early or too often = wrecked recovery + plateau, not faster gains. Save them for Building and Peak phases (weeks 6+) and last set only of isolation work.

Drop sets — when and how

WhatHowWhen to use
Standard drop setHit failure (or 0 RIR), drop weight 25-30%, immediately do max reps. 1-2 drops max.Last set of isolation only. Once per workout maximum. Never on compound lifts.
Mechanical drop setSame load, switch to easier mechanical position when fatigued (e.g. incline DB curl → standing DB curl)Better than load drops for some moves. Useful for biceps, lateral raises.
Triple dropFailure → drop → failure → drop → failurePeak phase only. Once a week max. Wrecks recovery — use sparingly.

Best uses in your program: Last set of leg extensions (B5), lateral raises (A4), tricep pushdowns (A7), preacher curls (C6). Skip on hack squat, hip thrust, lat pulldown — too much CNS load.

Rest-pause — when and how

WhatHowWhen to use
Rest-pauseHit failure, rest 15 seconds, pump out as many more reps as possible. Repeat 1-2 times.Compounds where extra mechanical tension matters. Last set of leg press, chest-supported row, hip thrust during Peak phase.
Myo-reps (Borge Fagerli)Activator set to ~RIR 1, rest 15 sec, do 3-5 reps, rest 15 sec, repeat 3-5 sets.Time-efficient density training. Good for short sessions.

Supersets — when they help, when they don't

TypeUse whenDon't use when
Antagonist superset (e.g. bicep + tricep)You want to save time. Performance often holds up.Compound lifts where you need full recovery between sets.
Agonist superset (same muscle, e.g. flat press + flyes)You want metabolic stress finisher. Last 5min of session.Strength-focused phases. Wrecks load capacity.
Pre-exhaust (isolation → compound, e.g. flyes → press)Trying to bring up a lagging muscle that's getting stolen by stronger synergists.You want to lift heavy on the compound — pre-exhaust kills load.

When intensity techniques are COUNTERPRODUCTIVE

  • Foundation phase (weeks 1-4) — you're building movement quality and base. Adding intensity techniques masks form errors.
  • Reta-day fatigue — Mon and Thu post-Reta are low-recovery days. Don't push to failure on these.
  • If sleep was poor — drop them entirely that session.
  • On compound lifts (squat-pattern, press, hip thrust) — failure form on these = injury risk. Stay 1-2 RIR always.
  • If you're plateaued — counter-intuitive: a plateau means you're already over-doing it. Deload first, then re-add intensity.

13. Weak Point Specialisation

What this is: The ability to bring up a lagging body part without overtraining everything else. The mistake most people make is adding 8 sets of biceps when biceps are weak — that wrecks recovery globally. Smart specialisation is targeted, not maximalist.

Step 1: Identify your real weak points (not just what you THINK is weak)

  1. Stand in the mirror, neutral pose. Take photos: front, side, back. Honest assessment.
  2. What looks small relative to the rest? That's a real weak point. Common ones for 48yo intermediate males: rear delts, hamstrings, upper chest, lateral delts.
  3. What looks fine? Don't add volume there.

Step 2: Apply the specialisation rule

ActionDetail
Add 4-6 sets per week to the weak muscleNOT 10. NOT 12. Four to six is the sweet spot.
REMOVE 2-3 sets from a different muscleIf chest is overdeveloped, drop 3 sets from chest to fund the new shoulder volume. Total weekly fatigue stays constant.
Place specialisation work FIRST in the sessionFresh = best growth stimulus. Weak chest? Start the workout with chest, not finish.
Use the reps you respond to (test it)Some lifters grow on heavy 6-8s for a stubborn muscle. Others grow on 15-20s. Try one phase of each, see which moves the needle.
Specialisation block = 8 weeks maxThen return to balanced programming. Specialisation indefinitely creates new imbalances.

The honest truth about your goal: +5kg of pure lean muscle in 12 weeks is unrealistic for any 48-year-old intermediate, even on TRT + HGH + Mast. Realistic 12-week target: 1.5-2.5kg of lean tissue + 1-2kg fat loss = visibly more "ripped". Patience compounds — 2kg of lean tissue four times a year = 8kg/year, life-changing.

Protein distribution (matters as much as total)

  • Spread protein across 4-5 meals of 30-50g each.
  • Each meal hits the leucine threshold (2.5-3g leucine = ~30g whey or 40g lean meat) — triggers maximum muscle protein synthesis.
  • Pre-bed slow protein matters at 48 — 30g casein or 200g Greek yoghurt. Overnight muscle protein synthesis without it drops significantly.
  • Post-workout protein within 2 hours = optimal but not urgent. The 30-min "anabolic window" was overstated by old research.

17. Critical caveats

  • STOP any exercise that reproduces low-back pain. Not soreness — pain. Especially nerve-type pain (shooting, tingling, numbness in legs). Sub the exercise out and message a physio.
  • This program assumes your eGFR is normal (creatine artifact, not real CKD). If cystatin C confirms real CKD, drop volume to 3 sets per exercise across the board, skip optional cardio finishers, and protein drops to 1.3g/kg as per the meal plan. Talk to your nephrologist before continuing if confirmed.
  • If Hct climbs above 0.52 — pause heavy training, get phlebotomy first. High Hct + heavy lifting = clot risk.
  • Reta-day fatigue is real. Mon and Thu (after Sun/Wed Reta) = lowest energy. Schedule those as upper days where possible (less systemic load than legs).
  • Not coaching credentials. This is structured programming based on evidence-based hypertrophy principles, your profile, and your stated constraints. Hire an in-person coach for 1-2 sessions in week 1 to check form on the key lifts (hack squat, hip thrust, chest-supported row). Worth the $200.
  • Re-test bloods at week 6 and week 12. Hct, eGFR, ApoB, full hormone panel. Adjust the program based on data.
